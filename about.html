Pai, Thailand · The people behind it

Built by people who learned the hard way

Sasha and Ying Goldsmith run Claude Camp on their organic farm outside Pai. Sasha built his first working app in 9 days using Claude Code — no coding background. Ying makes sure everyone is fed, comfortable, and can actually focus.

Sasha — how Claude Camp started

Sasha came to Claude Code the same way most people do: skeptical, curious, and with a project he'd been putting off for years. Nine days later he had a working, deployed website selling borosilicate lampworking instructional videos — something that had felt impossible the week before.

The experience was disorienting. Not because it was hard, but because the bottleneck had completely shifted. Writing code was no longer the constraint. Knowing what you wanted to build was the constraint. That gap — between having an idea and having something real — had just collapsed.

Claude Camp is built on that 9-day experience. The program exists to give other people the same compressed discovery: a structured week, a working app, and the clarity that comes from actually shipping something.

9 days to first app
7 participants per cohort
2 tracks — founder & dev

Sasha teaches both tracks at Claude Camp: the non-technical founder track, where no prior coding knowledge is assumed, and the developer track, where the focus is on how Claude Code changes the way experienced engineers actually work. The common thread in both is getting something built and deployed before you leave.

Ying — the reason the place works

Ying Goldsmith grew up in northern Thailand and has cooked Thai food her entire life. At Claude Camp she cooks all meals from produce grown on the farm — the kitchen produces more than the household needs, so guests eat what's in season, picked that day. Her food is the reason people stop talking about their apps at dinner.

Beyond cooking, Ying runs everything that keeps a retreat functioning: the bungalows, the schedule, the details that make a place feel easy to be in rather than effortful. Claude Camp has a high focus-to-friction ratio because of her.

The place

The farm sits on the outskirts of Pai, in Mae Hong Son province — Pai Canyon is directly across the road. The bungalows are open-air with no walls, built for the views and the climate. There are three standard bungalows and one penthouse bungalow, all with mountain sightlines.

The setup is deliberate. Good food, a comfortable place to sleep, the ability to get somewhere when you need a change of scene — these things matter when you're asking people to work hard for a week. The hot springs across the road help too.

Why 7 people

Each cohort is capped at 7 participants. That's not a marketing constraint — it's the number where Sasha can give everyone enough attention without the dynamic becoming a classroom. Small enough that people actually talk to each other. Large enough that you're not working in isolation.

Separate tracks for non-technical founders and experienced developers means the pace is right for the person in front of you, not some average of the group. By the end of the week, both groups ship something. The projects are different; the outcome is the same.
